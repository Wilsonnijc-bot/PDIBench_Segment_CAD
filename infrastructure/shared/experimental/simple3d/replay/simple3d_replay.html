How Simple3D measures the anomaly

Simple3D compares local 3D geometry descriptors with descriptors from the chosen normal reference: d(f) = min_j ‖f − p_j‖₂. Each prototype match is chosen in feature space. A higher score means the observed local geometry is farther from its nearest reference prototype. The replay reads the authors’ saved point scores directly.

1Local geometryFPFH describes normals and neighborhood geometry. MSND combines the authors’ neighborhood scales.
2Reference prototypesLFSA pools features around sampled centers. The official 5% coreset retains 204 reference descriptors.
3Nearest-prototype distanceEach observed pooled descriptor receives its minimum Euclidean distance to the normal prototype library.
4Point anomaly mapThe original interpolation and spatial smoothing map these distances back to every sampled observed point.
5Frame / video scoreOfficial MiniShift frame score = mean of the 80 largest point scores. Our video summary = mean of successful frame comparisons.

Red points are the top 80 scalar contributors. The other points use muted green. Red shows high relative anomaly evidence in this frame; the experiment has no calibrated binary deformation threshold. Geometry, reference assumptions and interpretation

Each cloud uses the existing object/link5 mask on a fresh 10-frame MegaSaM reconstruction, with invalid depth and isolated outliers removed. The authors’ centering and unit-radius normalization and the same sampling policy are applied to CAD and observed clouds. No rotation, ICP or non-rigid registration is applied. The reference and observed clouds are shown separately; their orientation and partial surfaces can differ.

For link5, switching CAD ↔ first observed frame changes the reference while keeping the exact same observed clouds. The first frame is scored in CAD mode and is the unscored normal reference in first-frame mode. Task-object mode uses the first selected object frame as its reference. Only selected frames are replayed; geometry and scores are never interpolated across missing frames. Failed comparisons retain their reasons and have no substituted score.

High scores can reflect deformation, MegaSaM noise, partial views, viewpoint changes, occlusion, mask errors, or the CAD-versus-reconstruction domain gap. RGB, masks, references and point scores are shown together to expose these effects. Human labels come from “Selected 45”, AB (Forearm deformation) or AF (Object deformation (0/1)2), in selected_45_matched_videos_styledv2.xlsx.
